# About the project

Netflix's strategy competition The Devil’s Plan is back for a third season, The Devil’s Plan: Dead Again. What would happen if AI agents entered the competition? Could they outperform human players? This project follows the main and prison matches from Season 3. Watch to see which of the 16 AI players comes out on top.

## 2. What is this project?

In this unofficial simulation, 16 AI agents play the games from Season 3 of The Devil’s Plan using the rules from the show. Some people start by thinking through a strategy, while others start by finding allies. What will the agents do? How will their personas, play styles, and earlier results affect the outcome? Could an alliance of weaker agents beat a stronger one? The replays follow the agents' conversations and actions so you can see how their choices affect cooperation, competition, and survival, and find out who wins.

## 3. Who are the 16 AI agents?

The AI agents are fictional contestants, each with a distinct persona and play style. They decide for themselves how to strategize, when to cooperate, and how far to trust others. One agent might propose an alliance right away, while another might think through a plan before acting. Their conversations and choices reflect these tendencies. They update their affinity and trust scores based on what other contestants do and what happens as a result. Agents keep their IDs, personas, and relationship scores across matches.

## 4. How much of the show has been recreated?

The project recreates the games as they air. It currently includes the main and prison matches from Days 1 and 2, which were released in the first week of Season 3. As in the show, each main match determines a winner, who goes to the Living Quarters or the Prison Wing, and who is eliminated. Contestants assigned to the Prison Wing play the prison match. The remaining players and their Pieces carry over to the next match. Later matches are planned as the season is released.

## 5. What can I see besides the final standings?

You can follow each player's choices, strategy notes, negotiations, and actions as the game unfolds. See who moved to a room first, whom they shared information with, whom they stayed on good terms with, and what they ultimately chose to do. In one game, P01 and P02 discussed where to place their tiles in Room 2, but both ended up placing them somewhere else. Their conversations, actions, and private notes show how their plans changed.

## 6. How does the simulation differ from the show?

On the show, human contestants hear the rules directly, interact in real time, and memorize information without making notes when a game requires it. Here, AI agents receive the rules, clues, and conversations they can see as text or images, then decide what to say, where to move, or what to choose. They can talk and act within the limits set for each phase.

For instance, when forming teams for Rectangle Counting in Either/Or, agents have up to 10 negotiation turns, with one opportunity to act per player each turn. The first three turns are for negotiation only. An agent who receives a team proposal in the same room can respond on their own turn. From turn four, agents can privately lock in their choice of solo or team play. Rectangle Counting begins as soon as everyone submits. If anyone still needs to submit at the end of turn ten, those players make their final choices simultaneously. In Memory Poker, contestants on the show study 16 cards for 20 seconds. In the simulation, agents see all 16 cards and choose 8–10 positions to memorize. After the three shared cards are revealed, agents can revisit only the positions they memorized. They can still guess at unmemorized positions when selecting their final two cards.
